title: Sourdough Starter
description: Maintaining a flour-and-water culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria, and reading when it is ready to bake with.
status: published
---

A starter is a stable culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria living
in a paste of flour and water. The yeast provides the lift and the bacteria
provide the acidity; a healthy starter is a negotiated balance between the
two rather than a monoculture of either.

Feeding is what maintains that balance. Discarding most of the culture and
replacing the volume with fresh flour and water resets the food supply and
keeps the population in its active phase. A starter fed once a day at room
temperature stays ready to bake with; one kept in the fridge slows to a
weekly feed and needs a couple of feeds at room temperature before it will
raise a loaf reliably.

Readiness is judged by behaviour, not by the clock. A starter is at peak
when it has roughly doubled and the surface has just begun to dome before
collapsing. Past that point the yeast has exhausted the available sugars and
the culture turns sharply more acidic.
