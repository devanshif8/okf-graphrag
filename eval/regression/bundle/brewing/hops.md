---
title: Hop Bitterness
description: How alpha acids isomerise during the boil to produce bitterness, and how boil time controls it.
status: published
---

Hops contribute bitterness through alpha acids, which are not themselves
especially bitter until heat converts them. During the boil the alpha acids
isomerise into iso-alpha acids, and it is those that the tongue reads as
bitterness. The conversion is slow, so boil time matters more than quantity
for bittering: a small hop addition boiled for sixty minutes contributes far
more perceived bitterness than a large one boiled for five.

This is why hop additions are described by their timing. A bittering
addition goes in at the start of the boil. A flavour addition goes in with
fifteen or twenty minutes left, long enough to isomerise a little but short
enough to keep some of the volatile oils that carry hop character. An aroma
addition goes in at flameout or later, contributing essentially no
bitterness at all.

See also [Brewing Water Chemistry](water-chemistry.md), since sulfate
sharpens the perception of bitterness independently of how much actually
dissolved.
