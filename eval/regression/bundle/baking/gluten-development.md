---
title: Gluten Development
description: How kneading and rest build the gluten network that traps gas and gives bread its structure.
status: published
---

Wheat flour contains glutenin and gliadin. Once hydrated, these link into
gluten, an elastic network that traps the carbon dioxide fermentation
produces. Without that network the gas escapes and the loaf stays dense.

Kneading speeds the process by forcing the proteins into contact, but time
alone will do it. An autolyse — flour and water rested together before salt
and starter go in — develops considerable gluten with no work at all, which
is why high-hydration doughs are usually folded rather than kneaded.
