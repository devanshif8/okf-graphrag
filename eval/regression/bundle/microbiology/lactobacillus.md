---
title: Lactobacillus
description: The lactic acid bacteria that acidify fermented foods, and the difference between homofermentative and heterofermentative strains.
status: published
---

Lactobacillus is a genus of bacteria that metabolise sugars into lactic
acid. That acidification is what preserves the food — most spoilage
organisms cannot tolerate the resulting pH — and what gives fermented foods
their sourness.

Strains divide into two useful groups. Homofermentative strains produce
lactic acid and essentially nothing else, giving a clean, sharp sourness.
Heterofermentative strains also produce acetic acid and carbon dioxide,
which reads as a more complex and vinegary sourness. A cold, slow sourdough
fermentation favours the heterofermentative strains, which is why a
retarded loaf tastes tangier than one proved warm.
